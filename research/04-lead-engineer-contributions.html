What a lead engineer could contribute at 01Resolved

The centerpiece analysis. This would almost certainly be the first senior engineering hire alongside one technical founder — owning architecture, not a slice of it.

Ordered roughly by strategic weight:

1. Make data correctness the moat (trust)

Everything they sell is a derived number: NAV, runway, pass edge, classifications. After the Ownership Score snapshot, incorrect individual scores were surfaced publicly on X. In an intelligence business one wrong number costs more than ten missing features.

Build: reconciliation against onchain truth, idempotent backfills, reorg/program-upgrade resilience, anomaly detection on derived metrics, a published data-quality methodology. "The numbers are right" becomes the brand, the way it is for Bloomberg.

2. Real-time infrastructure (speed)

Proposals go from announcement to live trading within hours; the Umbra defense flipped the market inside a 15-minute window. Signals exist, but only as Telegram pushes.

Build: streaming indexing (Geyser/websocket subscriptions instead of polling), live pass-edge feeds, webhooks in the API. "Is this proposal passing right now" is a product traders will pay for.

3. Generalize ingestion beyond MetaDAO (scale)

They already track launches on Futardio, Star, and Spark; stated scope is "MetaDAO and emerging launchpads." Today each protocol likely means bespoke pipeline work.

Build: an adapter architecture where a new launchpad, DAO framework, or eventually chain is configuration plus a thin adapter. This is the difference between covering one ecosystem and owning the category as futarchy spreads.

4. Productize the API into revenue

Beta, hand-keyed over Telegram, paid tiers merely "available," yet the docs already pitch six integration use cases to builders.

Build: self-serve keys, usage metering, billing, SLAs, status page. The API is the natural first revenue line and it compounds — every dashboard built on it deepens the moat.

5. OpenBooks as the B2B wedge (revenue)

Their own essays argue transparency infrastructure is the ecosystem's missing piece, and the July monthly's "Call" — route 100% of project revenue through DAO treasuries under a published policy — manufactures demand for exactly this product. The classification engine (rules/patterns/batches/periods) is already scaffolded in the app.

Build: a proper ledger engine — double-entry modeling of onchain movements, period close, auditable financial statements teams can publish. Every ownership-coin team is a prospective customer; a reporting standard also strengthens MetaDAO itself.

6. Turn one-off research into repeatable products (leverage)

The Ownership Score study (115K wallets, quartile retention) and Umbra forensics were labor-intensive one-offs; they've promised 1-week and 1-month re-runs — a treadmill by hand.

Build: internal analysis infrastructure — cohort trackers, wallet-forensics tooling, holder-quality scoring as a service. Research velocity doubles and the tools become sellable (e.g., holder-quality reports for teams planning raises).

7. Governance-attack early warning (new product)

The Umbra attack was narrated after the fact, but the attacker's pattern — fresh wallet, bridge funding, accumulation to exactly the proposal-stake threshold — is detectable in advance.

Build: funding-path tracing, accumulation alerts, proposal risk scores. Makes 01Resolved load-bearing infrastructure for every DAO treasury in the ecosystem, not just analytics.

8. Engineering leadership itself (multiplier)

Reliability gaps (production 500s), manual processes, client-rendered public site — normal two-person-startup debt, exactly what a lead engineer retires.

Do: set practices (testing, CI, observability, incident discipline), exploit the agentic-dev workflow they've already adopted, own hiring for the next engineers, and free the founders for what only they can do: research, distribution, ecosystem relationships.
